Mathematical model and conventions

(1) Y i , j = b + ∑ u = 0 k − 1 ∑ v = 0 k − 1 K u , v X ~ is + ud , js + vd

Here X̃ is the zero-padded input, s is stride, d is dilation, and b is bias. The equation describes the operation performed by Conv2d for a single channel and one filter.

Discrete mathematical convolution flips the kernel along both axes. For symmetric filters the result can remain unchanged; for directional detectors, the sign or orientation may change.

How do you interpret a filter?

Blur / averaging

Reduces local variations and high-frequency noise; also attenuates fine details. In modern models, filters are usually learned from data.

Sobel / gradient

Highlights intensity transitions in a particular direction; useful for understanding edge detection and the first convolutional layers.

Laplacian / sharpen

Responds to rapid changes or amplifies detail; may increase sensitivity to noise.

Feature map ≠ RGB image

Activations may be negative, exceed the interval [0, 255] and require normalization only for visualization.

05 / REDUCING RESOLUTION

Max Pooling versus Average Pooling

Compare what information each strategy preserves. A maximum can highlight a strong activation; an average integrates the responses in the window.

Properties and limitations

Y i , j = max u , v ∈ Ω X [ is + u , js + v ]

Max pooling is a local aggregation without learnable weights. For a fixed k×k window, average pooling uses the arithmetic mean. The examples assume no padding and ceil_mode=False.

When to use max pooling?

When the strongest activation is informative and reduced resolution is acceptable.

When to use average pooling?

When context aggregation and reducing local variation are important; global average pooling compresses each channel into a single value.

Alternative

Stride-2 convolutions, adaptive pooling or specialized downsampling mechanisms. Pooling is not mandatory in every CNN.

Receptive field recurrence

r l = r l − 1 + ( k l − 1 ) d l j l − 1
j l = j l − 1 s l r 0 = j 0 = 1

r: theoretical receptive field; j: distance between the centers of two adjacent receptive positions, measured in input pixels. Padding shifts the centers but does not change the size of the theoretical receptive field.

In practice, the effective receptive field may be smaller than the theoretical support of the architecture.

Usefulness: for segmentation, dilated/atrous convolution increases context without further reducing feature map resolution; for large images, the balance between context and detail matters.

Why does a residual connection help?

In a residual block, we do not learn H(x) directly alone, but the transformation F(x) added to the input. Observe the effect on the value and derivative of an educational scalar example.

Identity + residual

y = F ( x ; W ) + x ∂ y ∂ x = ∂ F ∂ x + I

The identity term provides a direct path for signal and gradient propagation. It does not guarantee that the gradient cannot vanish in any configuration; in real models, normalization, activations and dimensional projections also matter.

When is it useful? Very deep networks and backbones for classification, detection or segmentation (ResNet-18/34/50/101).

How do we choose upsampling?

H out = ( H in − 1 ) s − 2 p + d ( k − 1 ) + p out + 1

Transposed convolution is the linear operation associated with the transpose of a convolution matrix (in a matrix representation). It is not an inverse that automatically recovers the original image.

In the simulator: kernel 2×2, padding=0, output_padding=0, dilation=1. For stride=1, contributions overlap and are summed; for stride=2, coverage differs.
